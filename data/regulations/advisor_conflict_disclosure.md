# Advisor Conflict of Interest Disclosure Standards
**Regulatory Authority:** Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA)
**Instrument:** NI 31-103 — Registration Requirements, Exemptions and Ongoing Registrant Obligations
**Amendment Reference:** CSA Staff Notice 31-362 (August 2024)
**Applies to:** All registered investment advisors and portfolio managers

---

## 1. Purpose

This guidance clarifies disclosure obligations for registered advisors with respect to compensation arrangements that may create conflicts of interest. CSA Staff Notice 31-362 (August 2024) updates and strengthens requirements previously issued under NI 31-103 s.13.4.

## 2. General Conflict Disclosure — s.13.4

Registrants must take reasonable steps to identify existing and reasonably foreseeable material conflicts of interest between the registrant (including its representatives) and clients. All material conflicts must be:
1. Disclosed to clients in writing
2. Disclosed at or before the time the conflict arises
3. Described in terms the client can reasonably be expected to understand

## 3. Compensation-Related Conflicts — Tightened Standard (2024)

### 3.1 Performance Bonuses and Incentive Pay

**Prior guidance (2019):** Performance bonuses could be disclosed by reference to a compensation schedule or appendix that described the bonus structure in general terms.

**Current standard (August 2024 — Staff Notice 31-362):** Performance bonuses exceeding **$10,000 CAD** per year must be disclosed to affected clients using:
- Plain language description (not regulatory boilerplate)
- Specific dollar range or formula, not just "performance bonus may apply"
- Explanation of how the bonus could affect the advice given
- Opportunity for client to ask questions before proceeding

**The "reasonable client" standard:** Disclosure is considered adequate only if a reasonable retail client — not a sophisticated institutional investor — would understand the conflict from reading the disclosure.

### 3.2 Third-Party Compensation

Referral fees, trailer fees, and any payment received from third parties in connection with client recommendations must be disclosed:
- Before the recommendation is made
- With the specific amount or percentage
- With a description of how the advisor's compensation is affected

### 3.3 Commission Structures

Tiered commission structures (where higher asset levels generate higher commission rates) must be disclosed in plain language, including the threshold amounts and the advisor's current position within the tier structure.

## 4. Review of Prior Disclosures

Registrants are required to review existing client disclosure documents against the August 2024 standard. Any disclosure document prepared before August 2024 that covers performance bonus arrangements must be assessed for adequacy under the updated standard.

**If a prior legal opinion concluded that existing disclosure was adequate:** That opinion should be re-evaluated in light of the tightened plain-language requirement. A legal opinion based on the pre-2024 standard may not correctly characterize compliance with the current standard.

## 5. Enforcement Posture

CSA enforcement staff have indicated in 2024 that advisor compensation disclosure is a priority area. Recent enforcement actions have involved:
- Written warnings for boilerplate disclosure that did not meet the reasonable client standard
- Fines of $25,000–$75,000 per violation for systemic inadequate disclosure
- Conditions on registration where disclosure practices were found inadequate

## 6. Remediation Timeline

Registrants identified as having inadequate performance bonus disclosures must:
1. Update client disclosure documents within 90 days of identification
2. Re-disclose to affected clients in writing
3. Maintain records of re-disclosure
4. Self-report to the applicable SRO if systemic issues are identified
